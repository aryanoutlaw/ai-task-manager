# Priority levels

Every task has exactly one priority: low, medium or high. Priority describes impact and urgency, not effort. When in doubt, choose the lower priority and raise it later if needed.

## High priority

High priority means the work blocks users, customers, revenue or other teammates, or there is a security or data-loss risk. Examples: a bug that prevents users from logging in, a production outage, a security advisory, a customer escalation. High priority tasks must be picked up within one business day and should have a due date. Each person should hold no more than three high priority tasks at once; if you have more, escalate in the team channel.

## Medium priority

Medium priority is the default. It covers planned work that matters this sprint but does not block anyone today, such as a feature improvement, an intermittent bug with a workaround, or documentation needed for an upcoming launch. Medium priority tasks should be started within the current week.

## Low priority

Low priority is for nice-to-have work with no deadline pressure: refactoring, cosmetic polish, ideas and clean-up. Low priority tasks are reviewed during weekly triage and may be closed if they stay untouched for 60 days.

## Medium versus high

The difference between medium and high priority is whether something is blocked right now. If a user, customer or teammate cannot proceed, or there is a security or data risk, it is high. If the work is important but nobody is blocked and a workaround exists, it is medium.
